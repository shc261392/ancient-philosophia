## Overview

<Cited status="green">
Linear B is a syllabic writing system used to write **Mycenaean Greek**, the earliest known form of Greek. The tablets were found at palatial centers in Crete (Knossos) and mainland Greece (Pylos, Mycenae, Thebes, Tiryns). Michael **Ventris** deciphered Linear B in 1952 with help from John Chadwick, proving that the Mycenaean palaces were Greek-speaking — a revelation that fundamentally changed our understanding of Greek prehistory.<Ref id={3} />
</Cited>

## The Writing System

<Cited status="yellow">
- **~87 syllabic signs** — Each sign represents a consonant-vowel (CV) syllable: *pa, pe, pi, po, pu*, etc.
- **Logograms/ideograms** — Pictographic signs for commodities (wheat, olive oil, horses, chariots, textiles)
- **Number system** — Decimal, with signs for 1, 10, 100, 1000
- **Limitations:** Cannot represent consonant clusters, final consonants, or distinguish /r/ from /l/ — so a single sign can represent multiple sounds<Ref id={1} />
</Cited>

## Mycenaean Greek Features

<Cited status="yellow">
- **Labiovelars preserved** — *qe* for the labiovelar /kʷ/ that became π, τ, or κ in later Greek
- **Digamma preserved** — /w/ still present: *wo-i-ko* = οἶκος "house"
- **Case system** — Preserves the instrumental case lost in later Greek
- **Vocabulary** — Many familiar Greek words: *wa-na-ka* = ἄναξ "lord", *pa-te* = πατήρ "father"<Ref id={3} />
</Cited>

## Key Discoveries

<Cited status="yellow">
- **Knossos tablets** — ~3,000 tablets from Arthur Evans's excavations (written c. 1375 BCE)
- **Pylos tablets** — ~1,200 tablets from Carl Blegen's excavations (written c. 1200 BCE, just before destruction)
- **Thebes tablets** — Important finds including ritual texts
- **Mycenae and Tiryns** — Smaller but significant collections
</Cited>

## The Ventris–Chadwick Decipherment

<Cited status="green">
Michael Ventris's announcement on the BBC Third Programme on 1 July 1952 — and the subsequent joint publication with John Chadwick in the *Journal of Hellenic Studies* in 1953 — demonstrated that Linear B encoded a Greek dialect roughly 500 years older than Homer.<Ref id={8} /> Ventris's working notes are preserved in the Department of Classics at Cambridge; Andrew Robinson's biography reconstructs the technical breakthroughs (the 'grid' method, the recognition of place-names ending in *-i-jo*).<Ref id={9} /> Thomas Palaima's overview article in *Aegean Linguistic Heritage* surveys post-decipherment refinements.<Ref id={10} />
</Cited>

## The Pylos Linear B Archive

<Cited status="green">
The single largest body of Linear B comes from the **Palace of Nestor at Pylos**, where Carl Blegen's 1939 excavation recovered roughly 1,100 inscribed clay tablets in the so-called Archives Complex (rooms 7 and 8). The tablets were sun-dried administrative records that survived only because the catastrophic fire that destroyed the palace c. 1200 BCE accidentally baked them. Bennett's *Pylos Tablets* (1955, with second edition 1973) and the *Companion to Linear B* volume on Pylos texts remain the authoritative editions; Palaima and Killen have shown that the corpus, although purely administrative, lets us reconstruct the Mycenaean palace economy in extraordinary detail — landholding categories, religious offerings to *Po-se-da-o* (Poseidon) and *Di-we* (Zeus), bronze-workers' assignments, even watch-rosters in the final destruction year.<Ref id={2} /><Ref id={7} /><Ref id={10} />
</Cited>
