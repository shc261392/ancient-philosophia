## Overview

<Cited status="green">
The Old Kingdom (Dynasties 3–6) represents ancient Egypt's first age of monumental achievement. During this period, the divine kingship reached its most absolute form, the centralized bureaucracy was perfected, and the great pyramids were constructed — projects of staggering ambition that remain among the most iconic structures ever built by human hands.<Ref id={3} />
</Cited>

## Key Rulers

<Cited status="yellow">
- **Djoser** (3rd Dynasty) — Commissioned the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, designed by the architect **Imhotep** — the first monumental stone building in history
- **Sneferu** (4th Dynasty) — Built three pyramids (Meidum, Bent, Red), pioneering the true pyramid form
- **Khufu (Cheops)** — Builder of the Great Pyramid of Giza, the tallest man-made structure for nearly 4,000 years
- **Khafre** — Built the second Giza pyramid and likely commissioned the Great Sphinx
- **Menkaure** — The third and smallest Giza pyramid
- **Unas** (5th Dynasty) — First pyramid to contain the **Pyramid Texts**, the oldest religious corpus in the world<Ref id={5} />
</Cited>

## Society & Administration

<Cited status="yellow">
The pharaoh was considered a living god, the embodiment of Horus and son of Ra. A sophisticated bureaucracy administered the Two Lands, with the *vizier* (tjaty) as chief minister. Provincial governors (*nomarchs*) administered the nomes (provinces). The corvée labor system mobilized thousands of workers for pyramid construction — recent evidence suggests these were not slaves but organized labor crews housed in workers' villages.<Ref id={3} />
</Cited>

## Religion

<Cited status="yellow">
The solar cult of Ra at Heliopolis was paramount. The Pyramid Texts reveal complex beliefs about the afterlife: the king's *ka* (life-force) and *ba* (personality) would ascend to join the circumpolar stars. The pyramid itself served as a vehicle for the king's resurrection and eternal existence.
</Cited>

## The Pyramid Texts (Dynasty 5–6)

<Cited status="green">
First carved on the walls of Unas's pyramid at Saqqara around 2350 BCE, the **Pyramid Texts** are the oldest substantial body of religious writing in the world. The corpus comprises ~759 distinct "spells" or "utterances" — incantations, hymns, ritual instructions, and mythological allusions whose function was to guarantee the king's safe passage through the Duat (afterworld) and his apotheosis among the imperishable stars.<Ref id={6} /> The Allen translation makes the entire corpus accessible to English readers; the spell numbering follows Sethe's foundational edition.<Ref id={7} />
</Cited>

## Workers, Logistics, and the Pyramid Economy

<Cited status="green">
Mark Lehner and Zahi Hawass's three decades of excavation at Heit el-Ghurab ("the Lost City of the Pyramid Builders") south-east of the Giza plateau have overturned the slave-labour stereotype: the workforce lived in organised barracks, ate beef and bread at state expense, and rotated in *phyles* of roughly two thousand. The site preserves bakeries, breweries, copper-tool workshops, and a mortuary precinct for workers who died on site.<Ref id={8} /> Pierre Tallet's 2013 discovery of the **Diary of Merer** at Wadi al-Jarf — the earliest surviving papyri — records the daily logistics of transporting Tura limestone casing blocks to the Great Pyramid under the inspector Merer.<Ref id={9} />
</Cited>

## Collapse and the First Intermediate Period

<Cited status="yellow">
The Old Kingdom ended with the First Intermediate Period (c. 2181–2055 BCE), a time of political fragmentation, provincial autonomy, and possible climate stress associated with the **4.2-kiloyear aridification event** documented in Nile delta sediment cores.<Ref id={10} /> Power devolved to the nomarchs, and competing dynasties ruled from Heracleopolis and Thebes. The literary *Admonitions of Ipuwer* and the *Instructions for Merikare* — though composed later — preserve cultural memory of this collapse.<Ref id={1} />
</Cited>

## The Pyramid Texts of Unas (primary source)

  refId={11}

<Cited status="green">
The **Pyramid Texts** were inscribed inside the burial chambers of the late Fifth- and Sixth-Dynasty pyramids to assist the deceased king in his ascent to the sky. They constitute the world's oldest substantial religious literature — several centuries older than the Mesopotamian Gilgamesh and over a millennium older than the Hebrew Bible. Utterance 217, addressed to the sun god Rēʿ, identifies the dead king Unas with the imperishable circumpolar stars; the same body of spells later evolved into the Coffin Texts of the Middle Kingdom and the Book of the Dead of the New Kingdom.<Ref id={11} />
</Cited>
